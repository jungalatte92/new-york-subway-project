Welcome to The New York Subway Project!

Hello! Welcome to The New York Subway Project, which was previously funded by The Center of Digital Humanities Center at Texas A&M University from 2023-2024 with the support of the Technical Assistance Award provided from there. I am Jungah Kim, the owner of this website and this website explores the concept of the (invisible) New York Subway Laborers who were predominantly ethnic minorities that have been not as recognized as depicted, just as the Chinese American Laborers who worked on the Transcontinental Railroad. The inspiration to this digital project came from my reading of Patricia Park’s Re Jane in which the protagonist, Jane Re, a Korean American, struggles to racially identify herself in the New York subway which is surprisingly diverse, but is not completely accepted as part of the United States of America.

Navigating the Website

The website was mainly created due to the lack of accessibility found in finding resources on Asian American laborers who may have been hindered representation in the New York Subway creation, just as the Chinese Americans in the Transcontinental Railroad. Hence, I have gathered materials both offline and online that allow a general audience to navigate resources from The Museum of China in America, The New York Transit Museum, and The New York Public Library. The website consists of photos, a historical timeline of events that are related to the described New York Laborers research, newspaper rhetoric on Asian Americans on the New York subway, and a tentative census of Chinese Americans who may or may not have been part of the New York Subway laborer creation.

Significant Scenes from Patricia Park’s Re Jane on the New York Subway

“I stared at the other slumped passengers. The faces repeated in a pattern: Korean, Hispanic, Chinese, Chinese again, Indian. You could always tell by their worn experiences that they were going home from work.”

– Patricia Park‘s Re Jane

“My colleague’s doing some fascinating research on Queens. She’ll just ride your train for hours.”

– Patricia Park’s Re Jane

“The 7 is doing its usual rickety-racket routine. I’ll ride this train to its final destination, where Uncle Sang and Aunt Hannah will be waiting for me. My aunt will go tsk-tsk-tsk at me for wearing my dress on the train – a nunchi-less move, no doubt. My uncle will be double parked on the wrong corner of Roosevelt and Main, and we’ll fight about it the whole ride over to church. It’s high time that the MTA invested in some new train cars. But then I’d have to get to know a new train, and I’m certain some part of me would mourn the loss of the old, for all its flaws. After years of riding the 7, I’ve grown familiar with its herky-jerkiness, learning to accept its particular rhythms instead of fighting against them – or running away. And to realize, despite it all, that it has good intentions. I’ve begun to feel a comfort in its clumsy rocking. We’ve weathered so much together these past two-plus decades. You might say we’ve developed a kind of jung” (338).

– Patricia Park’s Re Jane
